Title: TRANSFER LEARNING SYSTEM FOR LOW-DATA VISUAL DEFECT DETECTION IN
       INDUSTRIAL MANUFACTURING WITH GRADIENT-WEIGHTED SPATIAL LOCALISATION

================================================================================
ABSTRACT
================================================================================

A computer-implemented system and method for detecting and localising surface defects
in manufactured components is disclosed. The method employs a convolutional neural
network (CNN) initialised with weights pre-trained on a large general-purpose image
dataset and fine-tuned on a domain-specific manufacturing quality dataset comprising
fewer than 500 labelled images. Fine-tuning is performed in two phases: an initial
phase in which only the final classification layers are trained while earlier
convolutional layers are frozen, followed by a second phase in which all layers are
jointly optimised with a reduced learning rate. Spatial localisation of detected
defects is performed using gradient-weighted class activation mapping (Grad-CAM),
producing an interpretable heat map overlaid on the source image to guide corrective
manufacturing action. The system achieves defect detection accuracy exceeding 94%
on an internal benchmark with only 320 labelled training images, enabling deployment
in manufacturing environments where labelled defect data is inherently scarce.

================================================================================
CLAIMS
================================================================================

1. A computer-implemented method for detecting and localising surface defects in
a manufactured workpiece, the method comprising:
    acquiring, by at least one image sensor, a plurality of digital images of a
    workpiece, each image capturing a different surface region or viewpoint;
    extracting, by a feature extraction module implementing a convolutional neural
    network (CNN), a feature representation from each digital image, wherein the CNN
    is initialised with pre-trained weights obtained by training on a general-purpose
    image classification dataset comprising at least one million labelled images;
    fine-tuning the CNN on a domain-specific defect dataset comprising fewer than
    500 labelled images of defective and non-defective workpieces in a two-phase
    procedure comprising:
        a first phase in which all convolutional layers except the final two
        convolutional layers are frozen, and only the final two convolutional layers
        and a classification head are trained;
        a second phase in which all layers of the CNN are jointly optimised using a
        learning rate that is at least one order of magnitude lower than the learning
        rate used in the first phase;
    classifying each acquired image as depicting a defect or no defect based on the
    extracted feature representation; and
    generating, for each image classified as depicting a defect, a spatial
    localisation map indicating a location and spatial extent of the detected defect
    within the image, wherein the spatial localisation map is computed using
    gradient-weighted class activation mapping (Grad-CAM) applied to the final
    convolutional layer of the fine-tuned CNN.

2. The method of claim 1, wherein the general-purpose image classification dataset
is ImageNet-1k comprising approximately 1.2 million training images across 1,000
classes, and the CNN architecture is selected from ResNet-50, ResNet-101,
EfficientNet-B4, or Vision Transformer (ViT-B/16).

3. The method of claim 1 or 2, wherein the domain-specific defect dataset comprises
images capturing at least three distinct defect categories selected from: surface
scratches, material cracks, coating delamination, geometric dimensional deviations,
and foreign material inclusions.

4. The method of any of claims 1 to 3, wherein the spatial localisation map is a
two-dimensional heat map of the same spatial dimensions as the input image, wherein
each pixel value in the heat map represents a gradient-weighted contribution of that
spatial location to the defect classification confidence score.

5. The method of any of claims 1 to 4, wherein classifying each image further
comprises computing a defect confidence score p ∈ [0,1], and wherein the image
is classified as depicting a defect when p exceeds a configurable decision threshold
θ, wherein θ is set by an operator based on a desired trade-off between false
positive rate and false negative rate on a validation set.

6. The method of any of claims 1 to 5, further comprising:
    generating a structured defect report comprising: (i) an image identifier,
    (ii) the defect confidence score p, (iii) a defect category label, (iv) the
    spatial localisation map rendered as a JPEG or PNG overlay image, and (v) a
    recommended corrective action retrieved from a lookup table indexed by defect
    category;
    transmitting the defect report to a manufacturing execution system (MES) via
    an OPC-UA interface.

7. A quality inspection system for detecting surface defects in manufactured
workpieces, comprising:
    at least one image sensor configured to acquire digital images of a workpiece
    surface;
    a computing unit comprising one or more processors and a memory, the memory
    storing:
        a fine-tuned convolutional neural network (CNN) as obtained by the method
        of any of claims 1 to 3;
        executable instructions that, when executed by the processors, cause the
        computing unit to perform the classification and localisation steps of the
        method of any of claims 1 to 6; and
    a display interface configured to render the spatial localisation map as a colour-
    coded overlay on the acquired image of the workpiece.

8. The quality inspection system of claim 7, further comprising a robotic arm
configured to receive a rejection signal from the computing unit and divert defective
workpieces from the production line to a quarantine area.

================================================================================
DESCRIPTION (Selected Passages)
================================================================================

[0001] The present disclosure relates to automated visual quality inspection in
industrial manufacturing, and more particularly to a transfer learning approach for
convolutional neural network-based defect detection with spatial localisation under
scarce training data conditions.

[0009] A fundamental challenge in deploying deep learning-based defect detection in
manufacturing environments is the scarcity of labelled training data. Acquiring and
labelling images of defective parts is time-consuming and expensive, particularly
for low-frequency defect types. Standard deep CNNs require tens of thousands of
labelled images for reliable training from random weight initialisation, making
them impractical for most manufacturing inspection scenarios.

[0017] Transfer learning from general image classification models (e.g. ImageNet-
pretrained CNNs) addresses the data scarcity problem by leveraging visual features
learned from large datasets. However, naive fine-tuning (updating all layers
simultaneously) frequently leads to catastrophic forgetting of general visual
features and overfitting to the small domain-specific dataset. The two-phase fine-
tuning procedure of the present invention—freezing early layers in the first phase
and jointly optimising all layers with a reduced learning rate in the second phase—
has been found by the inventors to mitigate both catastrophic forgetting and
overfitting simultaneously, yielding an improvement of 11 percentage points in
detection accuracy on the internal benchmark compared to single-phase fine-tuning.

[0028] The Grad-CAM technique (Selvaraju et al., "Grad-CAM: Visual Explanations
from Deep Networks via Gradient-based Localization," ICCV 2017) computes the
gradient of the class score with respect to the feature maps of the final
convolutional layer. These gradients are global-average-pooled to produce neuron
importance weights, which are then used to produce a weighted combination of the
feature maps. The resulting activation map, when upsampled to input image resolution
and overlaid as a heat map, indicates which spatial regions most strongly contributed
to the classification decision. A key advantage of applying Grad-CAM to the final
convolutional layer specifically—as claimed—is that this layer retains the highest
spatial specificity while also encoding the most task-relevant semantic features
after fine-tuning.

[0035] In experimental validation on an internal dataset of metallic turbine blade
surfaces comprising 320 labelled training images (210 non-defective, 110 defective
across 4 defect categories), the two-phase fine-tuned ResNet-50 achieved 94.3%
classification accuracy and 89.1% defect localisation accuracy (measured as IoU > 0.5
between predicted and ground-truth defect bounding boxes). This significantly exceeds
the 83.2% classification accuracy achieved by the same architecture trained from
scratch on the same dataset, and the 88.7% achieved by single-phase fine-tuning.
