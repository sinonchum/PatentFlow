II. OBJECTION UNDER ARTICLES 52(1) AND 56 EPC
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

--- A. Preliminary Remark on Patentability of AI/ML Methods (Article 52 EPC) ---

A method implemented by a computer program is patentable under Article 52 EPC only
if it produces a further technical effect going beyond the normal physical interactions
between the program and the computer. The claimed method must be assessed for its
technical character in its entirety.

The claimed method is directed to detecting manufacturing defects—a technical problem
with direct industrial application. The computing steps serve to solve this technical
problem. The Examining Division accepts, for the purposes of this examination, that
the claimed method has sufficient technical character under Article 52 EPC. However,
the Applicant should be aware that this assessment may be revisited if the claims
are broadened in scope.

--- B. Lack of Inventive Step (Article 56 EPC) ---

Independent claims 1 and 7 lack inventive step over D1 in combination with D3
and D4.

ANALYSIS OF CLAIM 1:

D1 (US 2019/0065878) discloses a computer-implemented method for detecting defects
in manufactured components using a CNN, comprising:

  (a) Acquiring digital images of a workpiece using an image sensor
      (D1, [0034], claim 1(a));
  (b) Feature extraction using a CNN initialised with ImageNet pre-trained weights
      (D1, [0041]-[0043]: "We initialise the convolutional backbone with weights
      pre-trained on ImageNet-1k to leverage general visual feature representations");
  (c) Fine-tuning on a domain-specific defect dataset (D1, [0048]-[0052], claim 3);
  (d) Binary classification: defect / no defect (D1, claim 1(d), [0055]);
  (e) Defect localisation using a class activation mapping technique (D1, [0061]:
      "spatial attention maps are generated to highlight discriminative regions").

D1 thus discloses the entirety of the feature-extraction and classification steps.

DISTINGUISHING FEATURES: Claim 1 differs from D1 in:

  (i)  The fine-tuning is a two-phase procedure with layer freezing in phase 1 and
       joint optimisation with reduced learning rate in phase 2.
  (ii) The spatial localisation specifically uses Grad-CAM on the final convolutional
       layer (as opposed to D1's unspecified "spatial attention maps").
  (iii) The domain-specific dataset is limited to fewer than 500 labelled images.

OBVIOUSNESS:

Regarding distinguishing feature (i): The technique of progressively unfreezing
layers during fine-tuning ("discriminative fine-tuning" or "gradual unfreezing")
is a well-established method in transfer learning, documented extensively in the
literature. D4 (Yosinski et al., 2014) demonstrates experimentally that lower
convolutional layers contain general features while higher layers contain task-
specific features, providing the scientific basis for freezing early layers.
The skilled person, wishing to fine-tune a pre-trained CNN on a small dataset
without overfitting, would directly consult D4 and implement progressive unfreezing
as an obvious technical measure. No unexpected effect beyond what is predicted by
D4's experimental analysis has been demonstrated.

Regarding distinguishing feature (ii): Grad-CAM is explicitly disclosed in D3
(Selvaraju et al., 2017) as a method for spatial localisation of CNN class
activations using the final convolutional layer. D3 states: "Grad-CAM uses the
gradients of any target concept flowing into the final convolutional layer to
produce a coarse localization map." D1's "spatial attention maps" is a broader
disclosure that encompasses Grad-CAM as a specific implementation. The skilled
person would consult D3—a seminal and widely cited publication in computer vision—
as an obvious choice of spatial localisation technique to implement within D1's
framework. The use of Grad-CAM specifically on the final convolutional layer is
the standard application of the technique as taught in D3 and requires no inventive
step.

Regarding distinguishing feature (iii): Specifying that the training dataset
comprises "fewer than 500 labelled images" is a characterisation of the operating
conditions, not a technical limitation of the method itself. The method of claim 1
does not exclude datasets of 500 or more images; it merely defines a boundary
condition. Furthermore, the achievable accuracy with small datasets is a consequence
of the transfer learning approach (itself obvious over D1+D4) and the effectiveness
of Grad-CAM (obvious from D3), rather than an independent contribution of the
claimed method.

CONCLUSION: Claim 1 does not involve an inventive step over D1 combined with D3
and D4 (Article 56 EPC).

--- Analysis of Claims 2 and 3 ---

Claim 2 (ImageNet-1k dataset, ResNet-50/ResNet-101/EfficientNet-B4/ViT-B/16):
These are the most commonly used architectures and datasets in transfer learning
for image classification and represent standard choices in the field, as confirmed
by D1 ([0041]) and numerous textbook references.

Claim 3 (three distinct defect categories): This is a straightforward limitation
on training data content that does not add a technical feature beyond defining
experimental conditions.

--- Analysis of Claims 5 and 6 ---

Claim 5 (configurable decision threshold θ): Using a configurable threshold to
balance false positive and false negative rates in a binary classifier is a standard
ROC analysis technique that does not add inventive step.

Claim 6 (structured defect report + OPC-UA interface): Generating a report and
transmitting it via a standard industrial protocol (OPC-UA, IEC 62541) represents
straightforward system integration. D2 (EP 3 467 733, also by Siemens) explicitly
discloses integrating a defect detection system with a manufacturing execution system
(MES) via an OPC-UA interface (D2, [0047]-[0050], claim 5).

III. OBJECTION UNDER ARTICLE 84 EPC — CLARITY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

3.1 Claim 1, "general-purpose image classification dataset comprising at least one
million labelled images":

This feature defines the pre-training dataset by size but not by content. A dataset
of one million randomly collected images would not necessarily yield useful pre-
trained weights for defect detection. The Applicant is invited to clarify whether
the claim is intended to encompass any dataset of this size, or specifically datasets
of the type used for large-scale image classification (e.g. ImageNet-class datasets).

3.2 Claim 1, "at least one order of magnitude lower":

This expression is mathematically clear (factor of 10) but the Applicant has not
explained whether this refers to the absolute learning rate value or the ratio of
learning rates between phases. If the phase-1 learning rate is 1e-3 and the phase-2
learning rate is 1e-4, the ratio is exactly one order of magnitude; if both phases
use the same scheduler, the comparison may be ambiguous. The Applicant is invited
to specify the learning rate ratio unambiguously.

3.3 Claim 7, "fine-tuned CNN as obtained by the method of claims 1 to 3":

A product-by-process claim (a stored model "as obtained by" a process) is
permissible under EPC practice only when the product cannot be sufficiently
characterised by its structural features. The Applicant should confirm that the
fine-tuned CNN cannot be defined by its weight values or architecture directly,
or reformulate claim 7 as a pure method or system claim.

IV. INVITATION TO APPLICANT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The Applicant is invited to respond within FOUR MONTHS. The Examining Division
notes the following potential avenues for overcoming the inventive step objection:

1. The description at [0017] and [0035] reports a specific quantitative improvement
   of 11 percentage points in detection accuracy compared to single-phase fine-tuning,
   and 94.3% accuracy with only 320 labelled images. If the Applicant can demonstrate
   that this effect is unexpected and attributable specifically to the claimed two-
   phase procedure (and not merely to transfer learning in general), this may support
   an inventive step argument. The Applicant is invited to provide a comparative
   experimental declaration comparing the claimed two-phase fine-tuning against
   progressive unfreezing methods disclosed in the literature (e.g. D4).

2. Alternatively, the Applicant may consider limiting the claims to a specific
   application domain (e.g. metallic turbine blade surface inspection), specific
   CNN architectures, or a specific quantitative performance metric, provided these
   limitations have adequate support in the description.

3. The Applicant's attention is drawn to Rule 137(3) EPC regarding the admissibility
   of amendments.
