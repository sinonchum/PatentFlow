OBJECTIONS UNDER ARTICLE 94(3) EPC
================================================================================

The present application has been examined. It does not meet the requirements of
the European Patent Convention for the following reasons:

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
I. PRIOR ART DOCUMENTS CITED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

D1: WO 2018/128361 A1 (SAMSUNG ELECTRONICS CO., LTD.) published 12 July 2018.
    Cited portions: claims 1-8; paragraphs [0031]-[0055]; FIG. 4-6.

D2: EP 3 379 882 A1 (NOKIA SOLUTIONS AND NETWORKS OY) published 19 September 2018.
    Cited portions: claims 1-5; paragraphs [0018]-[0034]; FIG. 2-3.

D3: 3GPP TS 38.214 V15.2.0 (Release 15, June 2018), Section 5.1.2.1, Table
    5.1.2.1.1-2 (publicly available standard, not a patent document).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
II. OBJECTION UNDER ARTICLE 56 EPC — LACK OF INVENTIVE STEP
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Claims 1-6, 7-8, and 9 lack inventive step (Article 56 EPC) in view of D1 alone,
or alternatively in view of D1 combined with D2.

--- Claim 1 ---

D1 (WO 2018/128361) discloses a method for downlink scheduling in a New Radio (NR)
system comprising:

  (a) receiving DCI on a PDCCH in slot n comprising a time domain resource
      assignment field (D1, paragraph [0033], claim 1);
  (b) determining a timing offset K0 from a TDRA mapping table indexed by the
      TDRA field value (D1, paragraphs [0038]-[0041], FIG. 4);
  (c) determining starting symbol S and length L from the same mapping table
      (D1, paragraph [0042], Table 1);
  (d) receiving the PDSCH in slot n+K0 on the determined time-frequency resource
      (D1, claim 2, paragraph [0044]).

The Applicant argues that claim 1 is distinguished from D1 in that the TDRA mapping
table is "pre-configured by the network device via Radio Resource Control (RRC)
signaling." However, D1 explicitly discloses that the UE receives the TDRA mapping
table via higher-layer signaling (D1, paragraph [0036]: "the TDRA table may be
provided to the UE via RRC configuration messages"). The skilled person understands
that higher-layer signaling in the 3GPP NR context refers to RRC signaling, as
confirmed by D3 (TS 38.214, Section 5.1.2.1.1, paragraph 1: "If the higher layer
parameter pdsch-TimeDomainAllocationList is configured...").

The Examining Division therefore concludes that claim 1 does not involve an inventive
step over D1 alone (Article 56 EPC).

--- Claim 5 ---

Claim 5 further specifies that HARQ-ACK feedback is transmitted in slot n+K0+K1,
where K1 is from a PDSCH-to-HARQ feedback timing indicator in the DCI.

D1 discloses HARQ-ACK feedback timing in paragraph [0051]: "the UE shall transmit
HARQ-ACK feedback after K1 slots from the PDSCH reception slot." The use of K1 as
a DCI-signaled offset is an obvious implementation consistent with the 3GPP NR
framework (D3, Section 9.1.2). No unexpected technical effect results from this
combination.

--- Claims 2, 3, 4 ---

These dependent claims specify parameters (four-row table, DCI formats 1_0/1_1,
K0 value range 0-5) that represent straightforward design choices well within the
standard 3GPP NR framework as documented in D3, and do not contribute to inventive
step.

--- Alternative attack: D1 + D2 ---

Should the Applicant argue that D1 does not explicitly disclose RRC configuration
of the TDRA table, D2 (EP 3 379 882) discloses precisely this: "the base station
transmits the time domain resource allocation configuration table to the user
equipment via Radio Resource Control (RRC) message IE pdsch-TimeDomainAllocationList"
(D2, paragraph [0022]). Combining D1 with D2 would be obvious to the skilled person,
since both address the same problem of flexible PDSCH scheduling in NR.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
III. OBJECTION UNDER ARTICLE 84 EPC — CLARITY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Claim 7 (terminal device): The feature "a receiver configured to receive DCI" is
unclear because a receiver is typically a hardware component that passively receives
all transmitted signals. The claim should specify how the receiver is configured to
selectively process PDCCH-carried DCI, or alternatively reformulate as a software-
defined functional unit.

Claim 9 (network device): The expression "configure a TDRA mapping table...via RRC
signaling" encompasses the case where the table is not yet transmitted, which creates
ambiguity about when the configuration is deemed complete. The Applicant is invited
to clarify whether "configure" refers to generating the table, transmitting it, or
confirming its reception by the UE.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
IV. INVITATION TO APPLICANT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The Applicant is invited to file, within FOUR MONTHS of the date of this
communication, observations and/or amendments that overcome the above objections.

Specifically, the Applicant is invited to:

1. Provide arguments distinguishing claim 1 from D1, in particular with respect to
   the RRC configuration feature, citing specific passages from D1 that do not
   disclose this feature.

2. Alternatively, amend claim 1 to add a feature not disclosed in D1 (e.g., the
   specific mechanism by which K0 dynamically adapts to cross-numerology scheduling
   scenarios, as described in paragraph [0057] of the application).

3. Address the clarity objections in claims 7 and 9.

Should the Applicant fail to respond within the set time limit, the application may
be refused under Article 97(2) EPC.

The Examining Division remains available for an interview should the Applicant wish
to discuss potential claim amendments before filing a formal response.
