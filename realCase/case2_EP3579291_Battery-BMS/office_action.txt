II. OBJECTION UNDER ARTICLE 56 EPC — LACK OF INVENTIVE STEP
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Independent claims 1 and 7 lack inventive step (Article 56 EPC).

--- Analysis of Claim 1 against D1 ---

D1 (US 2019/0237813) discloses a battery management method for a lithium-ion pack
comprising:

  Feature (a): acquiring temperature measurements from a plurality of NTC thermistor
               sensors, one per cell (D1, [0026], claim 1(a));
  Feature (b): computing Tmax and Tmin across all cells and deriving a temperature
               difference ΔT (D1, [0031], FIG. 3 step S104);
  Feature (c): maintaining full charging current I_max when ΔT is below a threshold
               T_th1 (D1, [0034], claim 3);
  Feature (d): reducing charging current when ΔT exceeds T_th1 (D1, [0036]-[0038]).

D1 thus discloses features (a) through (c) of claim 1 in their entirety.

DISTINGUISHING FEATURE: Claim 1 differs from D1 in that:

  (i)  The current reduction is proportional: I_charge = I_max × (1 - α × (ΔT - ΔT1)),
       whereas D1 discloses a step-wise reduction to a fixed reduced current I_reduced
       (D1, [0037]: "the charging current is reduced to 0.5C").

  (ii) A second threshold ΔT2 triggers complete suspension of charging and activation
       of a dedicated thermal balancing circuit (claim 1, final paragraph).
       D1 discloses only a single threshold with one response action.

OBJECTIVE TECHNICAL PROBLEM: Starting from D1, the objective technical problem can
be formulated as: how to further optimise charging speed while maintaining thermal
safety margins.

OBVIOUSNESS ASSESSMENT:

With respect to distinguishing feature (i): The use of a linear proportional current
reduction in response to a deviation from a target setpoint is a standard control
engineering technique (proportional controller). Applying such proportional control
to battery charging current is an obvious design choice for the skilled person
in battery management systems, who is familiar with PID control theory. No unexpected
technical effect beyond what would be predicted from proportional control theory has
been demonstrated by the Applicant.

With respect to distinguishing feature (ii): D2 (EP 3 340 347) explicitly discloses
a two-threshold temperature management scheme for lithium-ion packs:
  - Below T1: full charging current (D2, [0018]);
  - Between T1 and T2: reduced charging current (D2, [0020]);
  - Above T2: charging suspended, cooling activated (D2, [0022], claim 2).

The combination of D1's proportional current reduction with D2's two-threshold
architecture is straightforward for the skilled person, both documents addressing
the same technical problem of thermal management during charging of lithium-ion packs.
The combination does not produce any unexpected synergistic effect.

Accordingly, claim 1 does not involve an inventive step over D1 combined with D2.

--- Analysis of Claims 2 and 3 ---

The specific numerical ranges for ΔT1 (3-8°C), ΔT2 (10-18°C), and α (0.05-0.15/°C)
represent routine engineering choices achievable by the skilled person through
ordinary experimentation. The application's description ([0017]) does not provide
evidence of an unexpected technical effect associated with these specific ranges
compared to adjacent ranges. D3 (CN 106532175) independently discloses threshold
values ΔT1 = 5°C and ΔT2 = 12°C in a directly comparable system (D3, claim 2),
confirming that these values represent common general knowledge in the field.

--- Analysis of Claim 5 ---

The SOC balancing feature of claim 5 (OCV-based SOC measurement, 5% ΔSOC threshold,
passive/active balancing activation) addresses a known problem in battery management
independently of the thermal gradient management of claim 1. Such SOC balancing
systems are well-established in the art (see e.g. D1, [0058]-[0061], which discloses
both SOC monitoring and cell balancing as part of the same BMS). The mere combination
of thermal gradient management with SOC balancing, without any interaction between
the two features producing an unexpected effect, does not confer inventive step.

III. OBJECTION UNDER ARTICLE 84 EPC — CLARITY AND SUPPORT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

3.1 Claim 1, current reduction formula:

The term "maximum rated current I_max determined by the state of charge (SOC) of the
battery pack" is unclear. I_max is conventionally understood as a fixed hardware
limit (the maximum safe current of the cells), not a value that varies with SOC.
The description ([0038]) refers to a "maximum rated current" without explaining
the SOC dependency. The Applicant is requested to clarify whether:
  (a) I_max is a fixed hardware constant; or
  (b) I_max is a SOC-dependent lookup value (e.g., from a charging profile curve),
and to amend the claim accordingly.

3.2 Claim 3, coefficient α:

The range "0.05 per °C to 0.15 per °C" is supported by the description only at
[0017] ("the inventors have found that...") without experimental data or comparative
examples. In accordance with Rule 42(1)(e) EPC, the application should contain at
least one example demonstrating the effect of the claimed range relative to values
outside the range. The Applicant is invited to provide such experimental data or
to remove the numerical range from the claim.

3.3 Claim 9 (computer-readable medium):

A non-transitory computer-readable medium per se does not have a direct technical
effect beyond storing data. The Applicant is invited to consider whether this claim
format is appropriate under Article 52(2)(c) EPC regarding computer programs, or
whether the claim should be reformulated as a system claim.
