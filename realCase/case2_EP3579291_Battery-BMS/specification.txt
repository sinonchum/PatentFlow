Title: ADAPTIVE THERMAL GRADIENT MANAGEMENT SYSTEM FOR LITHIUM-ION BATTERY PACKS
       WITH MULTI-STAGE CHARGING CURRENT CONTROL

================================================================================
ABSTRACT
================================================================================

The present disclosure describes a battery management system (BMS) and associated
method for controlling the charging process of a lithium-ion battery pack comprising
a plurality of cells. The system continuously monitors inter-cell temperature
gradients and dynamically adjusts the charging current in multiple discrete stages
based on predefined thermal gradient thresholds. When the temperature gradient
between the hottest and coolest cells exceeds a first threshold, the charging current
is reduced proportionally. When a second, higher threshold is exceeded, an active
thermal balancing operation is initiated to redistribute heat prior to resuming
full-rate charging. The invention enables faster average charging rates than fixed
conservative current limits, while preventing localised thermal runaway events that
degrade cell longevity and present safety hazards.

================================================================================
CLAIMS
================================================================================

1. A method for managing the charging of a battery pack comprising a plurality of
lithium-ion cells (C1...Cn), the method comprising:
    acquiring, by a battery management controller (BMC), a temperature measurement
    Ti from each of a plurality of temperature sensors (S1...Sn), each sensor Si
    being thermally coupled to a respective cell Ci;
    computing a temperature gradient ΔT defined as ΔT = Tmax - Tmin, wherein Tmax
    is the maximum temperature measured across all cells and Tmin is the minimum
    temperature measured across all cells;
    when ΔT < ΔT1, supplying a charging current I_charge at a maximum rated current
    I_max determined by the state of charge (SOC) of the battery pack;
    when ΔT1 ≤ ΔT < ΔT2, reducing the charging current I_charge to
        I_charge = I_max × (1 - α × (ΔT - ΔT1))
    wherein α is a current reduction coefficient and ΔT1 is a first thermal
    gradient threshold; and
    when ΔT ≥ ΔT2, suspending charging and activating a thermal balancing circuit
    to reduce ΔT below ΔT1 before resuming charging at I_max,
    wherein ΔT2 is a second thermal gradient threshold greater than ΔT1.

2. The method of claim 1, wherein ΔT1 is in the range of 3°C to 8°C and ΔT2 is
in the range of 10°C to 18°C.

3. The method of claim 1 or 2, wherein α is in the range of 0.05 per °C to
0.15 per °C.

4. The method of any of claims 1 to 3, wherein the thermal balancing circuit
comprises at least one of: a liquid cooling loop circulating coolant between cells,
a Peltier thermoelectric element array, or a forced convection fan array.

5. The method of any of claims 1 to 4, further comprising:
    estimating a state of charge (SOC) of each individual cell Ci based on an
    open-circuit voltage measurement OCV_i taken during a rest period of at least
    30 seconds;
    computing a SOC imbalance ΔSOC = SOCmax - SOCmin across all cells;
    when ΔSOC exceeds a balance threshold δ of 5%, activating a passive or active
    cell balancing circuit to redistribute charge from higher-SOC cells to lower-SOC
    cells before resuming full-rate charging.

6. The method of any of claims 1 to 5, wherein the temperature measurements Ti
are sampled at a frequency of at least 1 Hz and the charging current I_charge is
updated within 100 milliseconds of detecting a change in ΔT that crosses a
threshold.

7. A battery management system (BMS) for a lithium-ion battery pack comprising
a plurality of cells, the system comprising:
    a plurality of temperature sensors (S1...Sn), each thermally coupled to a
    respective cell;
    a battery management controller (BMC) comprising a processor and a memory
    storing threshold values ΔT1, ΔT2, and coefficient α, the processor configured
    to perform the method of any of claims 1 to 6;
    a charging circuit controllable by the BMC to set a charging current I_charge
    supplied to the battery pack; and
    a thermal balancing circuit activatable by the BMC to reduce inter-cell
    temperature gradients.

8. The battery management system of claim 7, wherein the thermal balancing circuit
is a liquid cooling loop comprising a pump, a heat exchanger, and a plurality of
coolant channels routed between adjacent cells.

9. A non-transitory computer-readable medium storing instructions that, when
executed by a processor of a battery management controller, cause the processor
to perform the method of any of claims 1 to 6.

================================================================================
DESCRIPTION (Selected Passages)
================================================================================

[0001] The present disclosure relates to battery management systems (BMS) for
lithium-ion battery packs, and particularly to adaptive charging current control
based on real-time inter-cell temperature gradient monitoring.

[0008] Lithium-ion cells are highly sensitive to temperature non-uniformity during
charging. When cells within a pack reach significantly different temperatures, the
higher-temperature cells undergo accelerated electrolyte degradation and lithium
plating, reducing cycle life and potentially triggering thermal runaway. Conventional
BMS solutions use a conservative fixed charging current limit (e.g., 1C rate) that
does not adapt to actual thermal conditions, leading to unnecessarily slow charging
when gradients are low.

[0017] The inventors have found that using a linear current reduction formula
I_charge = I_max × (1 - α × (ΔT - ΔT1)) provides an unexpectedly effective balance
between charging speed and thermal safety. Unlike binary switching (full current /
zero current), the proportional reduction smoothly prevents gradient escalation
without unnecessary charging interruptions.

[0025] A critical inventive aspect is the two-threshold architecture: the first
threshold ΔT1 triggers proportional current reduction (soft intervention), while the
second threshold ΔT2 triggers full charging suspension and active thermal balancing
(hard intervention). This staged approach is absent from prior art systems, which
use either a single threshold (causing excessive interruptions) or no gradient-based
control at all.

[0038] In a preferred embodiment, the temperature sensors S1...Sn are NTC
thermistors with a measurement accuracy of ±0.5°C, sampled by a 12-bit ADC at 2 Hz.
The BMC updates I_charge within one ADC sampling period (500 ms) of any threshold
crossing event.

[0044] The SOC balancing described in claim 5 is performed independently of the
thermal gradient management and operates during rest periods where the OCV has
stabilised. Combining thermal gradient management with SOC balancing enables the
system to address the two primary causes of heterogeneous cell aging simultaneously.
